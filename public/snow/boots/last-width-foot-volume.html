Last width & foot volume

Ski boot fit lives or dies on two measurements most buyers never check: last width and internal volume. Here's what they mean, how to measure your foot, and which boots tend to match — from a former ski-shop equipment buyer. For a personalized number, run the Boot Fit Engine.

What "last width" really means

The last is the internal width of the boot shell at its widest point — the forefoot — measured in millimeters. It's the single most important spec for whether a boot will fit your foot without pressure points (Blizzard-Tecnica size guide).

Last is always referenced at mondo size 26/26.5. So when a boot is listed as "100mm," that means the shell is 100mm wide at size 26.5 — not at your size (The Ski Monster). Adult boots typically range from about 95mm (race/narrow) to 106mm+ (extra-wide), with the vast majority sitting between 98 and 102mm (Outdoor Gear Lab, Level Nine Sports).

The 2mm-per-size rule

Here's the part that trips people up: the published last is at size 26.5. For every shell size up or down, the actual width changes by about 2mm. A "100mm" boot is roughly 98mm at size 25.5, 102mm at 27.5, and 104mm at 28.5 (The Ski Monster).

That means you can't compare your bare-foot measurement to the published 26.5 number directly — adjust for your mondo size first, then compare.

How to measure your foot width

  1. Stand on a piece of paper with your full weight on both feet. Trace each foot with a pen held vertically (PTO Ski).
  2. Measure the widest point — across the ball of the foot, from the outside of the big-toe joint to the outside of the pinky-toe joint — in millimeters.
  3. Do it at the end of the day, when feet are largest. Measure both feet and use the larger one.
  4. Add ~2mm per shell size above 26.5 (or subtract below) so you're comparing to the last at your size.

Aim for the boot last to be slightly wider than your bare-foot measurement — you need clearance, and a shop can always snug a roomy boot or punch a tight spot, but they can't easily shrink one that's too wide.

Foot volume, explained

Volume combines last width with instep height to describe how snug or roomy the whole fit is (The Pro Ski & Ride). Most brands label boots LV (low volume), MV (medium), and HV (high volume):

  • Low volume — narrow last + low instep. Race/performance feel: snug, responsive.
  • Medium volume — the most common starting point. Balanced for average feet.
  • High volume — wide last + high instep. Comfort/freeride feel: roomier, more forgiving.

If your foot is…

  • Narrow + low instep → LV boots: Tecnica Mach1 LV (98mm), Lange RX/RS (97mm), Salomon S/Max (98mm). Expect a snug, performance-oriented fit.
  • Average width + average instep → MV boots: Salomon S/Pro (100mm), Atomic Hawx Prime (100mm), Nordica Speedmachine 3 (100mm), K2 Recon (100mm). The widest range of options.
  • Wide + high instep → HV boots: Nordica Sportmachine 3 (102mm), Salomon S/Pro HV (102mm), Dalbello Panterra (100–102mm), K2 BFC (103mm), Head Formula (103mm). Roomier and more forgiving.

Shells can be punched or stretched at pressure points — heat-moldable shells like Tecnica CAS, Salomon Custom Shell, and Atomic Adaptive Fit are specifically built for a shop to widen (Bootfitters). Start with the closest last and let a bootfitter fine-tune.
