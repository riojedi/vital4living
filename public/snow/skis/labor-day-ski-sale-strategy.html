Labor Day Ski Sale Strategy — What To Buy Now, What To Wait On

By Dex Okafor · Equipment Selection & Geometry

Labor Day weekend is the unofficial start of ski-season prep, and the named blowout sales — Sniagrab, Ski-A-Rama, Powder Daze, and Ski Rex — are where last-year's hardgoods and outerware clear at their steepest discounts (SkierDeals). But not everything is a buy-now. This guide maps the 2026 events and their end dates, then sorts every category into a clear buy now vs wait verdict so you spend where the discount is real and hold off where spring will treat you better.

Why Labor Day is the first real discount window

Labor Day is the moment retailers clear last season's inventory to make room for the new model year. Clearance events over the weekend can offer equipment and gear for up to 90% off regular retail prices, making it the broadest discount window before the season starts (SkierDeals). It's also when several tent-sale and consignment events cluster: BlueZone Sports runs its 9th Annual Labor Day Tent Sale in South Lake Tahoe from Thursday, September 3 through Monday, September 7, 2026, advertising up to 80% off ski and snowboard gear (BlueZone Sports), and Crystal Mountain's Labor Day Weekend Tent Sale promises up to 70% off past-season gear plus a first look at 2026-27 equipment (Crystal Mountain).

Online, the picture is just as broad. evo's Labor Day Ski Sale collection lists thousands of marked-down items with a Lowest Price Guarantee and free shipping on qualifying orders (evo), and its ski page alone returns over 4,200 sale results (evo). Powder7's 11th-annual Sweaty Yeti Labor Day Blowout covers used skis, new skis, boots, clothing, and accessories warehouse-wide (Powder7).

The named 2026 events & end dates

The four headline sales each have a distinct window and end date. Sniagrab (Sports Authority, Colorado and Utah stores plus online) and Ski-A-Rama (Ski 'N See, Sandy, Utah, plus online) both close on September 7. Powder Daze (Christy Sports) runs through September 8 online, and Ski Rex (Ski & Golf) opens Friday, September 4 — with email-list early access on September 3 — and ends September 8 online while Colorado stores continue through September 20 (SkierDeals). BlueZone's tent sale and Crystal Mountain's tent sale both bracket the same weekend (BlueZone Sports, Crystal Mountain), and Powder7's Sweaty Yeti kicks off August 22 and runs through September 4 (Powder7).

What the discounts actually look like

Ski-A-Rama publishes the clearest category math: 50% off most last year's skis and snowboards, 50–60% off all last year's jackets and pants, 50–60% off all goggles, gloves, and mittens, and up to 70% off everything else — plus a free mount with any ski-and-binding purchase and up to $215 trade-in credit for old gear (SkierDeals). Sniagrab advertises up to 90% off across brands like Burton, K2, Salomon, Columbia, The North Face, and Spyder (SkierDeals).

Online retailers run their own Labor Day collections. evo advertises up to 50% off ski packages (evo) and lists thousands of individual skis, boots, and bindings marked down (evo). Christy Sports' clearance ski page shows current-season skis at 30% off (e.g. an Armada ARV 106 MadSteez 25/26 cut from $749.95 to $524.97), prior-year boots at 49% off (a Nordica SportMachine 3 100 from $449.99 to $225.97), and bindings at 30% off (a Salomon Stage 11 GW from $189.95 to $132.97) (Christy Sports). Sun & Ski's past-season clearance goes further, with a Lange LX 120 HV GripWalk boot at 52% off ($359.84 from $750) and a LINE Vision 96 at 50% off ($449.84 from $900) (Sun & Ski).

Powder7's Sweaty Yeti lists concrete ski prices: a Nordica Enforcer 100 at $429, an Atomic Bent 85 at $329.93, a Faction Prodigy 2 at $419.93, and a Salomon Shift Pro 130 boot at $579 (Powder7). And for cross-country and clothing shoppers, Cross Country Ski Headquarters runs 30–50% off closeout clothing from Kuhl, The North Face, Patagonia, Columbia, and others over the same weekend (Cross Country Ski Headquarters).

Buy now vs wait, by category

The framework is simple: buy now anything where last-year's inventory is being cleared at 50%+ off, and wait on current-model-year boots and standalone bindings, where Labor Day discounts are modest and a better window is coming.

Why current-year boots are a wait

Boots are the one category where Labor Day is not the best window for the newest models. Current-season boots get their deepest clearance in March, when Christy Sports runs its annual Ski-Ya-Later Sale from March 6–29, 2026 across 50+ locations in Colorado, Utah, Montana, and Washington, including all three Sturtevant's family stores in the Seattle-Tacoma area (Christy Sports). Christy's own sale page confirms the asymmetry: skis up to 50% off, ski boots up to 60% off, but ski bindings only up to 20% off (Christy Sports). So if you want this season's boot, hold for spring; if a prior-year shell fits, grab it now at 49–60% off.

Why bindings are a "buy if you need them"

Standalone bindings rarely get clearance-deep discounts because they don't go obsolete the way skis do. Skiers on r/COsnow report that 25–30% off is already a strong deal for new, popular bindings, and that the real savings come at end of season when rental and demo returns hit 60%+ off — or when you bundle a binding with a ski purchase and the shop waives the mount fee (r/COsnow). Ski-A-Rama's free mount with a ski-and-binding purchase is the clearest example of that bundle value (SkierDeals). If you need bindings now, buy them bundled; if you can wait, spring demo returns are the deeper play.

How to decide when yours is borderline

When a category sits on the line, the decision comes down to urgency and whether a better sale is close. If your current gear is failing or daily friction is already high, buying now usually wins even at a smaller discount; if the item still works and a clear sale period is near, waiting often improves value (WorthItCheck). A useful framework is to set a buy-now price (current price minus discounts you can actually redeem today), estimate a wait-price range for the next sale window, and then weigh the cost of waiting — direct costs, inconvenience, and the risk the item sells out (onsale.space).

For ski gear specifically, that maps cleanly onto the calendar. Last-year's skis, outerwear, and accessories have their best window right now, so the cost of waiting is low selection and likely stockouts. Current-year boots and standalone bindings have a known better window in March, so the cost of buying now is overpaying — wait, unless your current setup is unusable.
